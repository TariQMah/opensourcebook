# Scenes service: reference

The detail behind [`README.md`](README.md): how one video is made step by step, every tool, the voice, the fresh viewer, the shelf, the styles, the toolbox, the HTTP contract and the settings.

## How one video is made

The agent works the way it works in its own sessions: one turn, on adapters that declare `replacesWorkPrompt` replacing the agent's built-in coding prompt (`SCENES_KEEP_AGENT_PROMPT=1` keeps it, for comparing) and otherwise keeping its own instructions with Open Sourcebook's added, Open Sourcebook's tools, and three **skills**. There is no clock, no count of looks and no enforced order: it writes, builds, looks, renders the real video, watches it and changes it for as long as it judges worth it, until it finishes or the person stops it. The one exception: `finish` answers once per video with the facts from Open Sourcebook's own record when a step was skipped (never rendered; rendered but never watched nor its viewer report read; a viewer report that listed things with no scene changed since; a render still running with nothing seen), attaching phone frames and findings when the current cut is unseen or its manual report is unaddressed, and the next `finish` always goes through.

**The skills** (`skills/`), each a folder with a `SKILL.md` (a name and a line on when it helps, then the knowledge) and references beside it. In a workspace they are files in the agent's folder (`work/skills/`); everywhere, `read_skill` reads them. None of it is a rule. The pages that bear on every video come whole in the system prompt (`src/knowledge.ts`), since agents left to open them opened almost none: what viewers notice, the teaching skill with its four references, the make-a-video page, the shelf's pieces a line each, and the scene language for the video's style as reference. The rest (depicting, the cookbook, media, the worked session, simulation, whiteboard, worked maths) stay files to open. `SCENES_AGENT_KNOWLEDGE=index` goes back to the index alone. Each turn's log gives the system prompt's and first message's size in tokens (characters / 4).

| Skill | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `make-a-video` | What Open Sourcebook needs from a video and how the tools fit together; the scene language's whole guide, written for this video's style and frame from `languagePrompt()` (`references/scene-language.md`); answering as the depicter (`references/depicting.md`); the cookbook of worked scenes on unrelated topics (`references/examples/`); one worked session; simulations, whiteboards and worked maths. |
| `teaching` | How to explain so a person follows: the viewer first, slow the first time and faster after, setting up before acting, what is on screen at each word; and telling, demonstrating, art direction and what research knows about watching. |
| `media` | The voice and its pauses, pictures (archives, the web, made pictures, cut-outs, PDFs, video frames), the sound kit, and the agent's own tools when its workspace is on. |

1. **Write, render, watch.** A connector turn (no time limit; `keepAgentPrompt` false when the agent's adapter declares `replacesWorkPrompt`, unless `SCENES_KEEP_AGENT_PROMPT=1`) with a system prompt (what the work is, the skills, the few things Open Sourcebook needs, then the given pages) and a prompt with the title, brief, what the viewer knows, the length, the styles (when the style is `auto`) and the passages (ids, titles, sizes; their text through a tool). The tools:
   - `read_passages(ids)`: the text.
   - `read_skill({ skill, file? })`: a skill's `SKILL.md`, or one of its files.
   - `about_video({ title?, idea?, style?, styleWhy?, short? })`: what the video is, said whenever the agent knows or changes its mind; stating `idea` before beat 1 attaches the picture of the whole shelf.
   - `write_beat({ beat?, after?, narration?, cites?, visual?, onScreenText?, emphasis?, source?, depictions?, remove? })`: one beat, its words and its picture together. Quotes are checked word for word (spaces aside). The narration may hold `[pause N]`, which the scene reaches as holds (`@h1`, `@h1.end`). The voice speaks the words at once and the answer gives every word's number and time; the source is built at once on them, with what the engine could not build past or what it measured. Each `depict` line is answered by the agent in `depictions`.
   - `look({ scene, at? } | { scene, from, to })`: up to four stills as JPEG images (540 by 960) at the word numbers given, or how the beat opens, its middle and how it ends, each with what is said then, the words on screen and what was measured; or, with `from` and `to`, a strip of small frames over that stretch with the word being said and where the picture stands still. As often as the agent wants.
   - `render()`: the real video, made exactly as the person will get it (steps 2 to 4 below), in the background while the agent keeps working (one render at a time on this computer). It waits up to 80 s and answers directly with phone-size frames of the cut and top viewer-report findings (the length, where each beat is, the voice, the sound and its loudness), or continues in the background if it takes longer; or names beats that broke on the real word times. A render of a video that has not changed since the last one is not made again.
   - `watch({ from?, to?, at?, phone? })`: frames of the last rendered mp4, over the whole video, every half second over a stretch, or one moment, each with its time, its beat and what the voice says then or the pause it is in, and whether the cut is the video as it stands. With `phone`, each frame is 360 pixels wide, the size the narrowest phones held upright show it, four a sheet. It waits for a render still running. Before any render, each beat's opening and end from the builds.
   - `viewer_report()`: what a person watching the last render on a phone would notice, in plain words, each line with its time, beat and measured numbers, the most noticeable first, then what reads fine (`src/report.ts`). It reads the scenes as sampled when the cut was rendered (every piece of writing and every drawn thing, ten times a second) and the mp4 itself (how much of the picture changes, where the sound is silent), with the cut's word times: writing on screen too briefly to read, gone before the voice names it or arriving well after, writing smaller than a phone's 11 pt, cut by the frame, on other writing, under a shape or in the captions' band, the picture standing still while the voice goes on or a lot arriving at once, a beat that opens empty or cuts as something appears, gaps and silence in the voice, its pace, mostly empty frames and small subjects. Information only: nothing is blocked or scored. Every threshold is a named constant with its source in `VIEWER`.
   - `sound({ music, effects })`: the agent places the kit, each sound on a scene and one of its word numbers with a level and why: music beds (each plays until the next cue) and a few effects where something lands. Unknown sounds, scenes and words are refused; at render, levels are kept in range (effects -40 to -18 dB, music -80 to 0 dB over a -26 LUFS bed) and an effect within 1.5 s of the one before is left out. None chosen means voice only.
   - `find_piece({ want?, name? })`: the shelf of pieces (below): the pieces that fit what the agent wants to show, with their lines, settings and pictures; one piece whole with its program; or every piece by name. A search that finds nothing is noted in `pieces-wanted.log` in the data folder.
   - `save_piece({ beat, program, name, about, tags?, params?, replace? })`: keeps a program from a beat on the person's own shelf (`pieces/` in the data folder), with what code sees that would make it more reusable.
   - The toolbox, when this computer can run it (below): finding, making and keeping pictures, which any scene then shows with `image id asset=NAME`.
   - `finish({ note? })`: takes the video once every beat has a source that builds; everything else is the agent's call. When the agent's last render is of the video as it stands (a hash of the style, script, sources, voice times, sound and pictures), the job is done with that very file, and steps 2 to 4 are skipped. An agent that ends its own turn without calling finish still makes the video when every beat builds; a turn cut off from outside (a usage limit, a sign-out, an error) before finish never does: the draft is kept, the job fails with why, and a resume carries the writing on in a new turn, so no video is delivered that its agent never judged done.
   A scene's first state is on screen from its first frame (it is built as a cut, unless the agent gave that state its own `enter=`), and `@0` is the scene's first frame rather than the breath before its first word, so a cut never lands on an empty board. A build that shows little but a title at 0.4 s says the scene opens empty.
2. **Voice.** **ElevenLabs** (when `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` is set) reads each scene with the scenes around it as context and gives real word times; the voice is the style file's `voice` block unless a setting names another. Each scene's speech is cut from the quiet around it and set after a 0.25 s lead, with 0.45 s after it, and no word is timed outside the speech. What it spoke is kept in the data folder by words and voice. Without a key the video has no sound, on every system: `silent` gives the word times by the engine's estimator at about 146 words a minute (ElevenLabs measured 135 on real scenes, `test/fixtures/eleven-pace.json`), no audio, so scenes, captions and length work as with a voice, and the captions carry the words (pages of five words, where the style sets none, so each stays long enough to read). Nothing chooses this computer's own voice by itself: `SCENES_VOICE=say` turns on macOS `say`, a robotic voice for a quick test, where each sentence is spoken and its words share the measured time by the estimator's weights. **Speechify** (`src/speechify.ts`, when `SPEECHIFY_API_KEY` is set and no ElevenLabs key is) reads each scene and gives real word times from its speech marks; it has no neighbours as context. **Kokoro** (`src/kokoro.ts`) is a voice that runs on this computer with no key: a person installs it once (`npm run voice:kokoro`, into `voices/kokoro/`, which fetches its model) and turns it on with `SCENES_VOICE=kokoro`. It runs in its own process (`voices/kokoro/speak.mjs`), loaded on first use and kept; it is timed like `say`, a sentence at a time (a long one in phrases the model reads whole), at 0.7 of the model's own brisk pace, about 155 words a minute (`SCENES_KOKORO_SPEED`), and what it spoke is kept by its words so a rewrite speaks only what changed.

   **Choosing the voice.** The voice services are one list, `src/voices.ts`, each described in its own file (its label, hosted or on this computer, the key it needs and where to get one, or the command that installs it). The app sets the voice up through the routes below: a service, its key and one of the account's voices, kept in a private file (`SCENES_VOICE_FILE`, by default `voice.json` in the data folder; the launcher keeps it in `.opensourcebook/voice.json`), written 0600 and laid over the environment, so the app's choice wins and a `.env` keeps working. A key is checked with its service when it is given: one the service refuses is not kept, one out of credits is (the key is good). No answer ever carries a key, only whether one is set and its last four characters, and no log line names one. After a change the voice is built again at once; a video or an audio already being made keeps the voice it started with. A voice on this computer that needs a large download is never installed by the service: the app shows its command and checks again.

   **When the voice goes away.** A voice that cannot speak for a reason trying again will not fix (the account out of credits, the key or account refused, no answer through every try) throws `VoiceDown` (`src/voice.ts`). The agent is told once, as a fact, in the answer of the call that met it: why, that trying again will not help, and what happens next. The draft keeps `voiceDown`, the voice is not asked again for that video, every later beat is timed by the estimate and builds on it, `render` says it cannot run, and `finish` takes the video. The job then fails as `voiceDown` with its draft kept: the engine waits for the voice (asking `GET /voice`, free) and resumes it, which voices and renders it with no new turn when the writing was finished; or the person chooses to finish it without sound (`resume` with `withoutSound`), made like a video with no key, its voice note saying why it has none.
3. **Final build** of every scene on the real word times. In `render` a scene that breaks is named to the agent with its problems. After the turn (when the agent did not render the video as it stands), a scene that breaks gets one short repair turn with its problems; if it still breaks, the job fails with a plain message.
4. **Render.** Each scene is an engine clip, back to back, then an end card in the style, on screen as long as its words take to read (5 to 7 s): the video's title, under "Source" (or "Sources") each source by its name, even one named like the video, and under "Pictures" the credits of pictures from elsewhere: where the video came from, which is what a viewer asks at the end (the count of quotes and that each was checked is not on the card; the item's own page shows every cite at its claim), every line at least 36 pixels tall and laid out to fit (`video/end-card.ts`), what does not fit counted rather than cut; captions from every word's time (the engine's caption pages, burned in), a page never ending inside an abbreviation (H.R., U.S., e.g., Dr.) or between a short label and its number (S. 12), and the abbreviation keeping its stops; the voice joined, the music beds ducked under it, the effects (ducked more gently), the whole faded out over the last two seconds and mastered to -14 LUFS, -1.2 dBTP; a silent voice stays silent, with no audio track in the file. H.264 at 1080 by 1920, 30 fps.

   **On a small computer.** The frames drawn at once follow the computer (`pipeline/lib/frames-at-once.ts`): a share of its cores and memory, one at least and six at most, with `RENDER_CONCURRENCY` able to set it by hand. A render that lacks room (the renderer refuses the number, a frame times out, the browser runs out of memory) is tried again with fewer frames at once, down to one and then once more with more time, and the video's step says so in plain words. Chalk's dry strokes and words are painted with the tooth already in their colour (a tile of pixels as the paint) rather than masked, which looks the same: a mask made the browser draw every stroke into a frame-sized layer, and chalkboard rendered at a quarter of the pace of the plain styles.

   **How long it takes.** Every render adds its frames a second in its style to `speed.json` in the data folder, and the first time (a style with no speed) the service draws a short stretch of a sample scene in each offered style in the background to have a number at all (`src/speed.ts`). `/status` carries it as `speed`, the render's step says `Drawing the video, about 4 minutes on this computer`, and the engine's maker says the same in the plan row and the make tile. A style that has not been measured says nothing about time.

## The fresh viewer

Once the agent's writing is done, a second, blind turn of the person's agent watches the finished video the way a person meets it, and its notes go back to the maker for one round of polish (`src/review.ts`). It is a viewer's notes, never a gate: nothing is scored, graded, passed or failed, and the maker weighs them for itself.

1. **The cut.** The agent's last render when it is of the video as it stands; else the video is rendered once. A copy is kept in the job's folder as `review/cut-N.mp4`.
2. **The viewer.** A connector turn with no folder and none of the agent's own tools, so it cannot read the scenes, the script, the passages or any file, on the same path on every agent (Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode). It is told it is watching on a phone, does not know the topic and has not seen how the video was made. It gets the one-line topic (title and brief), the video's length against the length asked, the spoken words with their times and the viewer report's measured findings, and three tools: `watch_video` (the whole cut as frames about one a second, at most 72, each 360 pixels wide, nine a sheet on at most eight sheets, the most pictures the connector passes in one answer), `watch_frame` (up to three frames at full size, at the times it picks) and `submit_review`. It looks through five lenses: did it teach (what it learned in three to five sentences in its own words, where it got lost, whether the key mechanism was shown happening or only told), the picture (a recognisable mechanism in motion or boxes with words; the whole frame used, the lower half above the captions included), words, voice and picture together, readable and finished on a phone, and right and consistent. It answers with what it learned (`taught`), what worked (one to three strengths and the best moment, so they are kept), the faults with their times, the most important first, the weakest moment and the one change that would help most. An agent that writes its answer out instead of calling `submit_review` is read from its words (a JSON object, or the fields under headings). The turn may run 20 minutes.
3. **One polish turn.** With faults named, the maker gets one more turn, with its usual instructions and tools, the viewer's notes framed as one viewer's to weigh, and its beats as they stand (where each sits in the cut, its narration and its whole source, and the sound), so it changes only what it agrees with, renders and calls `finish`. What it ends with is the video, as after any turn.
4. **The video is never lost.** No faults, a viewer that could not answer (a limit, an error, its time out), or no cut to watch: the finished cut is handed over as it is. A polish turn cut off from outside puts the draft and the first cut back, and the person gets that.

One round a video, ever: `draft.json` keeps `review` from the moment it starts, so a restart, a resume or a revise never starts another (a remake of one scene is a new video, with a round of its own). The record of the making carries `review` (how it ended, the cut watched, the number of faults and how the answer came, and after polish the beats changed and the cut rendered last), and the log has the viewer's whole answer. On by default (`SCENES_REVIEW`); a request's own `review` wins.

## The shelf of pieces

A **piece** is a ready-made thing the agent can put on screen and drive with a few settings and the spoken words: a program of the scene language, drawn in the style's own colours, fonts and line. Each is one folder in `pieces/`: `piece.js` (the program), `piece.json` (a line on what it is, tags a search can meet, and each setting with its default and meaning), `example.md` (a narration and a scene that uses it, built by the tests in every style) and `still.jpg` (its picture on the shelf). The shelf is read from the folders, so a new piece is a new folder: the language guide lists it and `find_piece` finds it. The person's saved pieces live in the data folder's `pieces/`, in the same form, and are searched with the shipped ones. Search is a plain word match over names, tags, lines and settings, with no model call.

To make or change a piece, `npm run piece -- NAME --styles flat,ink,neon` builds its scene in those styles, prints what a viewer would notice, and writes stills and a sheet of them to look at; `--picture` writes its `still.jpg`. Programs have helpers for this work (`items`, `textWidth`, `fitSize`, `wrapText`, `said`, `beats`, `appear`; the language guide says what each does). A piece that draws from data, as the map draws from its outlines, keeps it beside its program in `data.json`, and the program reads it as `pieceData`; where the data came from and its licence go in the folder too (the map's `DATA.md`).

## Styles

A **style** is a small world, not a palette: one file in `styles/` says its palette and fonts, and in its `render` block its line (`line`: chalk, ink, marker, drafting, vector or neon pen, with wobble, taper, passes, grain, overshoot), its fills (`fill`: solid, hatch, scribble, watercolour wash) and page (`surface`: grain, smudges, light, a grid), its letters (`type`, including `halo`, a rim of what the words sit on so a line behind them never touches them), its motion (`motion`: the feel, how things enter, `write` for words written on from their start, and `cut`, how one scene gives way to the next: a cut, a dip through the page or the page wiped across), and its captions (`caption`: plain words, words with a rim of the page, or words on its caption strip). Its `design` block says how each role looks (title, label, key, answer...), what may sit on what, and its panels, pointers, highlights and pictures. Everything left out comes from presets, so a new style is one file of a few lines; `STYLE_GUIDE` in `engine/style/format.ts` is the whole format.

The engine never names a style: drawing (`engine/style/paint.ts`, `draw.tsx`), motion (`engine/core/compile.ts`), captions, cuts and the end card (`video/`) read only what the file says. Words are measured in the style's own fonts everywhere, by the layout, by programs (`textWidth`, `fitSize`, `wrapText`) and by the checks on built frames.

A style is **offered** unless its file says `"offered": false`: kept in the repo, it still draws any video that names it (an older plan's), but it is left out of what the person, the planner and the video agent choose from. Today twelve are offered; sketchnote and terminal are kept until every piece on the shelf builds in them with nothing measured that a viewer would notice (the shelf test checks each offered style). An offered style that does not hold up on finished videos is set back to `"offered": false` or removed.

## The toolbox

Tools the agent can reach beyond the scene language, in `src/toolbox/`. Each says what it is for; when to use one is the agent's call. A tool whose provider is missing on this computer is not offered, and the system prompt describes only the tools offered.

| Tool | What it does | Needs |
|---|---|---|
| `web_search({ query, n?, where? })` | Pages from Muse Spark's web search, or Wikipedia's own search | `META_API_KEY` for the web; Wikipedia needs nothing |
| `open_page({ url, from?, screenshot? })` | A page's title, words (12,000 characters at a time), links and pictures (as ids); a screenshot as a phone or a desktop shows it; pages built by scripts are read after they run | headless Chrome for screenshots |
| `find_images({ query, sources?, n? })` | Open archives (Wikimedia Commons, NASA, The Met, Smithsonian, Internet Archive, Openverse), public domain or openly licensed only, shown as small pictures with ids | `SI_API_KEY` optional (Smithsonian's shared key allows few searches an hour) |
| `view_images({ from })` | Ids, addresses or kept assets, larger | |
| `save_image({ from, name, about?, replace? })` | Keeps a picture as an asset, up to 2048 pixels, with its credit | |
| `generate_image({ prompt, name, shape?, references?, about?, replace? })` | A picture from words through a generator in `generators.ts` (Muse Image now), tall, square or wide, following up to 4 reference pictures | `META_API_KEY` |
| `video_frames({ url, at? })` | Frames of a video file, or of a video page through yt-dlp, at the moments asked (or six spread over it) | `yt-dlp` on the path (or `SCENES_YTDLP`) for video pages |
| `read_pdf({ url, pages?, pictures?, figures? })` | A PDF's pages as words, and as pictures or their embedded figures | |
| `cut_out({ asset, thing, name, replace? })` | SAM 3.1 finds the thing by name; it becomes a PNG asset with a see-through background | `META_API_KEY` |
| `assets()` | What is kept, with the pictures | |

Every picture the agent is shown gets an id (`c12`) it can keep by. Kept pictures live in the job's folder under `assets/`, which the render serves; stills inline them as small data URLs. A picture from elsewhere carries a credit line (maker, source, license) that the video shows under it while it is on screen and lists under "Images" on the end card; a generated one has none.

Every address is checked before it is fetched, and again after each redirect: only http and https, and never a host on this computer or a private network, so nothing the agent opens can reach the agent connector, this service or the person's own network. Downloads stop at a size limit (8 MB for a page, 40 MB for a picture, 60 MB for a PDF). Headless Chrome, ffmpeg and yt-dlp fetch the page or video at an address that passed that check.

## The workspace

Where the agent connector has its box (macOS; `services/agent-connector/README.md`, "A workspace"), each video's turn also gets the agent's own tools, its shell, files and web, in the video's folder `work/`, walled off from the rest of the computer and from your own logins (the agent signs in there with a token of its own; without it its own tools stay off). The system prompt then says so, and `save_image` keeps a file from there (`from: "work/chart.png"`, with `source` when it came from elsewhere). `SCENES_AGENT_WORKSPACE=off` keeps the agent's own tools off.

## Contract

Auth on every route except the turn routes: `Authorization: Bearer <SCENES_TOKEN>`. JSON unless noted.

| Route | |
|---|---|
| `GET /status` | `{ ok, maker: { id: "scenes", label, description }, agent: { reachable, id?, label?, model?, effort?, problem? }, voice: { id, label, real, note? }, styles: [{ id, label, description, offered }], speed?: { [styleId]: { framesPerSecond, setupSeconds, measuredBy } }, made?: [{ seconds, videoSeconds, agent?, model?, effort? }] }` (`voice.note` is the one line of what a person should know when there is no key or the voice is the test one, from `src/voice-choice.ts`, the only place that words it: the launcher's check, the engine's maker status, the Studio, the video's own mark and the `opensourcebook` answers all repeat it): every style, offered ones first (only offered ones are listed for choosing; a video may name any), plus recent `made` totals kept per `(agent, model, effort)` bucket. |
| `GET /voice` | `{ ok: true }`, or `{ ok: false, trouble: credits\|refused\|unreachable, message }`: whether the voice can speak now, read without speaking (for ElevenLabs, its subscription), so nothing is paid for. The engine asks it for videos waiting on the voice. |
| `GET /voice/setup` | `{ service?, voice?: { id, label }, from: app\|env\|none, note?, services: [{ id, label, where, about, needsKey, keyLabel?, keyHelpUrl?, keySet?, keyHint?, installed?, install?: { command, folder }, listsVoices, samples }] }`: the voice as set up, every service offered, hosted A to Z then local. `samples` says whether the service offers a sample of its voices to hear; when it does not, every voice in its list says `sample: false`. |
| `PUT /voice/setup` | `{ service: id \| null, voice?: id \| null }` → the setup. `400 unknown_voice_service`, `409 voice_needs_key`, `409 voice_not_installed`. `null` is no voice. |
| `PUT /voice/services/:id/key` | `{ key }` → `{ service }` with `health`. `422 key_refused` (nothing kept), `404` (no such service with a key). |
| `DELETE /voice/services/:id/key` | Forgets the app's key (a `.env` key still counts) → the setup. |
| `GET /voice/services/:id/voices` | `{ voices: [{ id, label, description?, language?, sample }] }`: the voices on the person's account. `409 voice_needs_key`, `502 voice_service_unreachable`. |
| `GET /voice/services/:id/voices/:voiceId/sample` | The voice's own preview (`audio/*`, `cache-control: private, max-age=3600`); `404` when it has none. A preview its storage serves as another type (ElevenLabs' come as `text/plain`) is sent with the audio type its own first bytes say (MP3, Ogg, WAV, FLAC, MP4, WebM); one that is not sound is `404`. |
| `GET /styles/:id/preview` | `image/jpeg`, 360 by 640, a frame of a fixed sample scene in that style; rendered once, kept in the data folder. 404 for an unknown style. |
| `POST /videos` | `{ id, title, brief, assumes?, frame: { width, height }, targetSeconds?, style: id or "auto", agent?, model?, effort?, passages: [{ id, sourceId, sourceTitle, locator?, text }], sources?: [{ id, title, kind, url?, path?, pictures?: [{ url, kind, caption?, page?, timestampMs?, width?, height?, contentType? }] }], limits?: { maxScenes, minScenes, narrationMaxChars, visualMinChars, visualMaxChars, onScreenTextMax }, review?: boolean, reviewAgent?, reviewModel?, reviewEffort? }` → `202 { id }`. Optional `agent`, `model`, and `effort` override the default agent pick for that video (omitted picks use defaults without cross-agent leakage). `review` turns the fresh viewer on or off for this video; left out, the service's setting decides. `reviewAgent` is who that viewer is, with `reviewModel` and `reviewEffort` for that agent only (sent without it, they are refused); left out, the viewer is the video's own agent, with `SCENES_REVIEW_MODEL`/`EFFORT` only when that is the service's own agent. `sources` is for the job's folder (`docs/JOB-FOLDER.md`): a link to each source that is a file on disk, and the pictures, only the engine's own `/v1/assets/<id>`, fetched from `SCENES_ENGINE_URL`; left out, the folder is made from the passages alone. The same id again returns the same job. `400 { error, problems }` lists what is wrong. |
| `GET /videos/:id` | `{ id, state: writing\|voicing\|rendering\|done\|failed\|stopped, progress, step, steps: [{ label, at }], style?, failure?: { code, message, detail?, limit?: { resetsAt?, resets? } }, result?, making? }` (`making`, until there is a result, is how the video was made so far) (`failure.limit`, with `agentLimit`, is when the agent said its plan's limit resets: an ISO time, its own words, each only when it said so); `draft` is whether its draft is still in the data folder, so one scene of it can be made again. |
| `GET /videos/:id/file` | the mp4 (409 until done) |
| `POST /videos/:id/stop` | stops the agent's turn or the render; state `stopped` |
| `POST /videos/:id/revise` | `{ problems: string[] }`, when `done` or failed with `scriptRefused`: a new turn hears the problems and starts from the draft. State back to `writing`. |
| `POST /videos/:id/remake-scene` | `{ id, beat, note?, agent?, model?, effort? }`, when `done`: a new video `id`, copied from this one's folder and draft (its beats, pictures, sound and voice files, not its cut, finish or fresh viewer's round), whose writing is a new turn told in the person's words which beat to make again and that the rest stays as it is → `202 { id }`. The old video is not touched and plays until the new one is done; the same `id` again is the same remake. A beat whose words do not change keeps its speech (nothing is spoken or paid for again); every frame is rendered again, since the render is one pass over the whole timeline with its voice, music and captions mixed through it. The new video gets its own fresh viewer, and `GET /videos/:id` says `remakeOf: { id, beat }`. Another `agent` (with its own `model` and `effort`) may be named; otherwise the old video's pick. `409 { error: not_done \| draft_gone, message }`, `404` for an unknown video or beat, `400` without `id` or `beat`. |
| `POST /videos/:id/show-files` | Opens the video's job folder in this computer's file manager (Finder, Explorer, or the desktop's own) → `200 { folder }`; `404` when the video or its folder is gone. |
| `POST /videos/:id/resume` | when `failed` or `stopped`: carries the video on from its draft, a new turn told what exists when the writing was not finished, else the voicing and render again → `200` the job as `GET /videos/:id` gives it. `409 { error }` while it is being made or once it is done; 404 for an unknown id. It clears `failure`, and does not count among the job's own carry-ons. `{ withoutSound: true }`, after the voice could not speak: the video is finished with no voice and its captions carrying the words, its voice note saying why. |
| `POST /turns/:turnId/tools`, `/finish` | the connector's callback for that turn, with the turn's own token |

`result` = `{ title, idea, scenes: [{ id, durationMs, narration, onScreenText, visual, cites: [{ passageId, sourceId, quote }] }], file: { bytes, durationMs, width, height, contentType }, voice: { id, label, real, note? }, by: { maker: "scenes", agent, model?, effort?, style }, making, timing: { makingSeconds, review?: { seconds, agent, model?, effort? } } }`. `timing` is how long the making took (the agent's turns, the voice, building and drawing; never a wait for a limit, the voice or the person) and the fresh viewer's own turn apart, with the pick it ran on; the engine keeps it as the time of that pick (`make_times`), and no time is said from anything else. `making` is how the video was made, step by step, written by Open Sourcebook from the tool calls it answered (`src/making.ts`), never asked of the agent: passages read, skill files read with read_skill, beats written and refused, looks, renders, whether a render was watched and the viewer report read, and of which cut, before or after finish's answer (`seen`, the one record finish's facts read too, so the two always agree), which render the person got (`handedOver`: the agent's cut by number, or `after`, one made after its turn), the beats changed after a report, the pieces searched for and used, the pictures searched for (find_images) and kept (save_image, generate_image, cut_out), sound, how often finish was called and whether it answered once with facts (that answer kept whole, `finishAnswer`, and logged whole as the call is answered), the fresh viewer's round when one ran (`review`), and every tool Open Sourcebook answered by name with its count. Each turn's end and the job's end also write it to the log. It cannot see files the agent read with its own tools in its folder. `failure.code` is `agentUnreachable`, `agentMissing`, `agentSignedOut`, `agentLimit`, `agentError` (the agent's turn ended in an error of its own before it called any of Open Sourcebook's tools, such as a model its account does not offer; never read as a refused script), `timedOut`, `stopped`, `scriptRefused` or `error`; `message` is plain words for the person, `said` the agent's own words with `agentError`, `detail` is for logs.

`progress` (0 to 1) never goes back within a round; a revise starts a new round from 0. `file.durationMs` includes the end card after the last scene.

Default limits (a caller's win): 2 to 12 scenes, 600 characters of narration a scene, visual 20 to 700 characters, at most 4 on-screen texts. Fixed: 120 characters per on-screen text, 4 cites a scene, 300 characters a quote.

Jobs live in the data folder, one folder each (`job.json`, `draft.json`, builds, stills, voice, `video.mp4`). A restart keeps finished jobs; a job that was being made is carried on from what is on disk, its step "Carrying on after a restart": the writing in a new turn told what exists (the style, each beat and whether it builds and was looked at, any cut), or the voicing and render again, where speech already made is never bought again. While a turn runs the connector is asked every 5 s whether it still does (`GET /turns/:id`); a turn whose connector has not answered for 30 s, or has said no longer runs for 15 s with no end arriving, is carried on the same way (a gap of over a minute between checks is the computer asleep, and is not counted). A job is carried on by itself at most 3 times, after 2 s, 15 s and 60 s, waiting up to 3 minutes for a connector that is starting again; then it fails in plain words and keeps what was made.

### Audio

A spoken walk-through with no picture (`engine/docs/audio.md` is the contract). The engine sends the parts its agent wrote; this service speaks each with the same voice videos use, joins them with half a second more quiet between parts than between sentences, masters the whole with the video's mastering (-14 LUFS, -1.2 dBTP, no music or effects, the fade-out on quiet after the last word) and encodes AAC in MP4. Same token as the video routes.

| Route | |
|---|---|
| `GET /status` | also `audio: { ready: true }`, or `{ ready: false, problem }` when the voice makes no sound (the problem says what to do) |
| `POST /audio` | `{ id, title, parts: [{ id, text }] }` (1 to 60 parts, ids `[a-z0-9-]`, unique, text at most 2,500 characters) → `202 { id }`. The same id again is the same audio unless it failed or stopped, when it starts again. `400 { error, problems }`; `409 { error: "no_voice", message }` without a voice. |
| `GET /audio/:id` | `{ id, state: voicing\|mastering\|done\|failed\|stopped, progress, step, failure?: { code, message }, result? }` |
| `GET /audio/:id/file` | the m4a, `audio/mp4` (409 until done) |
| `POST /audio/:id/stop` | stops it; state `stopped` |

`result` = `{ durationMs, bytes, contentType, voice: { id, label, real }, loudness: { lufs, truePeak }, parts: [{ id, startMs, endMs, words: [{ text, startMs, endMs }] }] }`; each part's words are its text split on spaces. `failure.code` is `noVoice`, `voiceRefused` (the voice's own message), `stopped` or `error`. One audio is voiced at a time; each lives in `audio/<id>/` in the data folder (`job.json`, the voice, `mix.wav`, `audio.m4a`), and a restart keeps finished ones.

## Trying it by hand

To try it without the engine, with a running service: `SCENES_TOKEN=… node --experimental-strip-types scripts/try-video.ts examples/heart.json out.mp4`. The examples (a body, a sky and a maths derivation with symbols) are self-written study notes, used only to try the service.

To hear a finished video with another voice: `node --experimental-strip-types scripts/revoice.ts .data/<job id>` voices it again with the voice set here, builds every scene on the new word times and renders it into `.data/<job id>-revoiced` (the job is not touched), and says how far each scene's words moved from the times its scenes were written on.

## Notes

- **Chrome is needed even to build a scene**, not only to render: text is measured in the browser that draws it. On macOS a Chrome kept inside Desktop, Documents or Downloads cannot load its own page (those folders are guarded per app), and renders wait until they time out. `npm run browser` therefore also keeps a copy in `~/Library/Caches/opensourcebook-scenes`, which the service uses unless `OPENSOURCEBOOK_CHROME` is set. A copy made before the rename, in `~/Library/Caches/re-scenes`, is used where it is and never moved.
- **Fonts ship in `fonts/`**, all under the SIL Open Font License, one folder per family with its licence and a `font.json` (see `fonts/README.md`). A style names families; measuring and drawing load the same files, so a scene lays out and looks the same on any computer. A style that names a font with no folder there fails with what to add.
- **Remotion licence.** Rendering uses Remotion 4, which has its own licence (`node_modules/remotion/LICENSE.md` after install): free for individuals, for-profit companies of up to three people and non-profits; a larger company needs a company licence, and reselling a derivative of Remotion itself is not allowed. More in [`docs/THIRD-PARTY.md`](../../docs/THIRD-PARTY.md).
- The instruments report and never judge; some readings are false alarms (low contrast on digits that read clearly, for example). The agent is told to weigh them.
